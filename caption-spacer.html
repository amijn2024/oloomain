Frequently Asked Questions

Why do Instagram captions lose their line breaks?
Instagram automatically removes empty lines when you post. This collapses your carefully spaced paragraphs into one block. Our tool inserts an invisible Braille pattern space (U+2800) on empty lines, so Instagram reads it as a non‑blank character and preserves the line break.
How do I use this caption spacer tool?
It’s simple: paste or type your caption into the box above, click the Convert & Format Caption button, and then copy the formatted text. Paste that version directly into Instagram, Facebook, or TikTok and your spacing stays intact.
Is there a character limit for Instagram captions?
Yes – Instagram allows up to 2,200 characters per caption. You can also include up to 30 hashtags. Our live counter shows exactly how many characters and hashtags you’ve used, so you’ll never hit the limit unexpectedly.
Will this work on Facebook and TikTok as well?
Absolutely. The invisible Braille character is a standard Unicode space that works on Facebook, TikTok, LinkedIn, Twitter/X, and most social platforms. Just paste the formatted caption wherever you write posts.
Why does the formatted caption contain an invisible character?
The tool replaces empty lines with the Unicode character “⠀” (U+2800, Braille blank). It looks empty to the human eye, but counts as a printable character. Social media algorithms treat it as text, so the line break before and after it is kept.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about this tool and its practical use.

Why do social captions lose spacing?

Some platforms normalize whitespace or handle pasted blank lines differently, which can compress carefully formatted text.

What does a caption spacer help with?

It helps prepare copyable spacing so separate parts of a caption remain easier to distinguish.

Can I use it for different social platforms?

Yes. You can use the formatted text as a starting point on platforms that accept normal text and line breaks.

Should I preview the caption after pasting?

Yes. The destination platform controls the final rendering, so a quick preview can catch unexpected spacing changes.
