How to estimate the speaking time of a video script

A script-length estimate is useful during planning because spoken words are one of the easiest production variables to measure before recording. Divide the word count by the planned words-per-minute rate to get a baseline duration.

For example, a 150-word script at 150 words per minute is about one minute of spoken delivery. Real videos can take longer because of pauses, demonstrations, scene changes, emphasis, and editing.

Why this workflow matters

Creators often need to know whether a script is likely to fit a target duration before recording. A words-per-minute estimate gives you a useful planning baseline.

Recommended workflow

  1. 1Paste or type the script.
  2. 2Choose a speaking pace that matches the intended delivery.
  3. 3Review the estimated duration.
  4. 4Add time for pauses, demonstrations, on-screen actions, or transitions when planning the final edit.

Practical tips

  • Checking whether a script fits a target duration
  • Planning voice-over length
  • Comparing slower and faster delivery styles

Important limitations

  • The result is an estimate, not a recording prediction. Pauses, emphasis, pronunciation, demonstrations, cuts, and individual speaking speed can change the final duration.

Helpful answers

Questions about this guide

Answers to common questions about this tool and its practical use.

How does a script length calculator work?

It uses the number of words in a script and an assumed speaking rate, usually expressed as words per minute, to estimate duration.

Is the estimate exact?

No. Real recordings vary because of pauses, emphasis, pronunciation, demonstrations, and individual speaking style.

What speaking rate should I choose?

Choose a rate that resembles the actual delivery style you plan to use rather than assuming every speaker talks at the same speed.
